01 · The rooms

A room for each thing you love.

Every medium has its own words. A film asks about the cut. A novel asks about the prose. A game asks about the feel. Same log, four vocabularies.

  • F
    Film

    The look, the cut, the score, direction, performances. Credited to the real crew.

  • T
    TV

    The season arc, the cast, writing, the world. Finished it or part way.

  • B
    Books

    The prose, the structure, the voice, the ideas. Paper or audio.

  • G
    Games

    The feel, the mechanics, the level design, the sound. Finished it or dropped it.

06 · Clubs

A programme with a deadline.

Thirteen horrors before Halloween. Every Le Guin. Six games before winter. Start one for your room, or open it to everyone. The ballot stays sealed until it closes — then the club produces its own best-of.

  • ◷
    Two kinds.

    A season ends on a date. A standing club has no deadline — finish it whenever, then run it again next year.

  • ✕
    The ballot is sealed.

    Everyone votes as they finish and nobody sees the standings. A count is all you get until it's called.

  • ★
    Then it opens.

    Ballots score 5-4-3-2-1 and combine, and the club has a list of its own — with a winner, and an argument.

08 · Questions
Isn't this just Letterboxd with more tabs?

Letterboxd tells you what someone watched and how many stars. Rooms tells you what they loved about it, how closely a crowd agrees, and how much their taste matches yours — across film, TV, books and games.

What's public and what isn't?

Your profile, your taste, your entries and ratings are public by default, and anyone can follow you. Any single entry can be switched to room-only. Rooms themselves are private and invite-only — the talk inside them never leaves.

How is taste match worked out?

From what you each single out, weighted by how highly you rated the thing, across all four mediums. Two people who both go for the ending and the world will score high even if they've never logged the same title.

Where does the data come from?

TMDB for film and TV, Open Library for books, IGDB for games. Your entries are yours: export them as JSON any time, and there's a local mode that never touches a server.
